Abstract

Excess fuel consumption has two very different causes with the same symptom: a driver who accelerates hard and a vehicle that is running rich both burn more than they should. We show that the two causes leave separable signatures. Driver-caused excess is transient and kinematic; malfunction -caused excess is steady-state and combustion-linked. We build an expected-fuel model on real fleet telemetry (Vehicle Energy Dataset, VED), decompose each trip's excess into a driver-behaviour component and a persistent vehicle-baseline component, and validate the behaviour component on an independent labelled driving dataset. On a controlled engine-fault dataset (EngineFaultDB) we show a rich-mixture fault produces measurable excess fuel with an emissions/AFR signature. A cross-dataset double dissociation establishes the central claim: a combustion axis, measurable in both worlds, explains the fault-caused excess yet is silent for driver-caused excess.

Contributions

  • A signature-based framework that attributes excess fuel to driver behaviour vs vehicle malfunction using only public data.
  • A variance decomposition of trip fuel on a 197-vehicle, 14.5k-trip public fleet into behaviour, vehicle-baseline, and environment components.
  • A cross-dataset double dissociation showing the combustion axis explains malfunction-caused excess (R² 0.90) but not driver-caused excess (R² 0).
  • External validation of the behaviour axis (driver-held-out AUC 0.96, permutation p 0.03) and an honest account of the field-wide absence of any joint fuel+fault+behaviour dataset.

4. Method

4.1 Expected-fuel model and excess

Per trip we predict fuel per 100 km from trajectory-derived driving features and trip conditions using gradient boosting, with out-of-fold predictions grouped by vehicle so no vehicle is scored by a model that has seen it. Excess is the positive residual against this expectation.

4.2 Two axes

Kinematic axis: transient aggression (positive acceleration, jerk, harsh-event rate, speed variability). Combustion axis: deviation of the air-fuel ratio from stoichiometric, measured as fuel trims in VED and directly as AFR/lambda in EngineFaultDB, placed on a common percent-fuel-per-air scale.

5. Arm 1: driver-behaviour decomposition (VED)

The expected-fuel model reaches R² = 0.673 (MAPE 13.3%) with vehicle-grouped cross-validation. Variance decomposition of trip fuel:

ComponentShare of fuel variance
Environment / route (distance, speed regime, temperature, weight)0.592
Driving behaviour (unique marginal over environment)0.081
Vehicle-baseline (persistent per-vehicle effect)0.142
Unexplained0.185

The vehicle-baseline is real and largely not size-driven: regressing the per-vehicle effect on engine displacement and weight explains only 23% of it. A gentle-driving counterfactual saves a median 0.47 L/100km (about 5% of median fuel), rising monotonically across aggression deciles.

5.1 Behaviour-axis external validation

On the independent Driving Events dataset, kinematic features separate aggressive from non-aggressive events with the driver held out: logistic-regression AUC = 0.958 (per-driver 0.98 / 0.93 / 0.99). A label-permutation test collapses this to chance (mean 0.482, p = 0.032), confirming the signal.

6. Arm 2: malfunction fuel signature (EngineFaultDB)

Training the fuel model on normal operating points and scoring faults at matched operating point, the rich-mixture fault burns +6.2% excess fuel (AFR 13.68 vs 14.07; brake-specific fuel consumption +10.6%). Lean and ignition faults are not excess-fuel faults. The rich-fault excess correlates with the combustion signature (CO, HC, AFR), not with kinematics.

7. Attribution: signature double dissociation

The central result. Excess is defined net of context in each dataset; each axis is regressed on the excess it is meant to explain.

AxisDriver-excess (VED)Fault-excess (bench)
Kinematic (transient aggression)0.107n/a (steady-state)
Combustion (AFR / fuel trim)−0.0390.903
The combustion axis explains ~90% of malfunction-caused excess and ~0% of driver-caused excess. The same axis that is diagnostic of a fault is silent for aggressive driving; the kinematic axis carries the driver signal. The two causes are separable by signature.

Note stated plainly: on the bench AFR and fuel are physically coupled, so the combustion axis explaining fault-excess is expected; the informative finding is the dissociation, that this axis is silent for driver-excess.

8. Scope and limitations

  • Driver and fault ground truths live in different datasets, so the dissociation is cross-dataset, not a single-trip competing-cause classifier. This is the boundary set by available public data.
  • VED has effectively one driver per vehicle, so per-trip driver-versus-vehicle causation is not identifiable at the unit level; the decomposition is model-based and validated by convergent external evidence.
  • EngineFaultDB is a controlled test bench with no driver context; the fuel-relevant fault is the rich mixture. The lean-versus-ignition label mapping is taken from the source paper's text.
  • Telematics are resampled to 1 Hz; fine transient dynamics are smoothed.
